Slide Design
Slides e.g. PowerPoint, Google Slides etc. are used extensively in professional settings, and part of our goal in this course is to empower you to become a more effective presenter using slides. Too often, presentations encourage lack-luster delivery or create redundancies that bore the audience. We encourage you to rethink how you use slide design so that it becomes a tool that enhances your delivery, instead of a distraction to your audience. Our persuasive presentation guidelines in the pre-class video will provide more specific suggestions and guidelines for our principles of slide design.

 

Given that you have five minutes our baseline recommendation is that you use approximately five slides. Use slides to support and enhance what you are saying. We do not want the slides to be the show. This class focuses on your speech content and delivery, and your authenticity.

 

With that said, we will expect that the slides you do show help to enhance your persuasiveness. In particular, we hope to see slides that are visual in nature (in other words, with minimal text and a very limited use of bullet points) and that communicate something to your audience that it would be difficult for you to describe in words (charts, images, and sound/video clips are all examples of ways to make your slides communicate in this way).

 

Slides should be compatible for presenting on PC and Mac. PowerPoint is not required but could be advisable as you cannot use animation on .pdf and even if you use Google Slides we will be downloading it so that we are not dependent on WIFI.

 

 A note on risk-taking: The strongest talks are rarely the pitches you’ve given a hundred times at work. They are deeply personal, grounded in storytelling and meaning, where data supports the emotional case. You lead in multiple arenas: your organization, family, and community. Choose a topic that reflects that fuller self. Students often play it safe now, and need to rework their talks later or change topics completely. We encourage you to take that risk now.

Speech Content
Please come to class with a prepared four to five minute persuasive presentation. This assignment is an individual persuasive presentation based on a current/timely issue of your choosing (such as a personal passion, a mission or cause you care deeply about, or charitable cause). You may also choose a real business issue you face, but you must be personally connected to and passionate about this topic. The speech should communicate your true beliefs; it is NOT a role-play.

 

For either personal or business topics, do NOT simply present the issue or raise awareness, but instead you must create a call to action for the audience you will face in class.

 

Your grade will be based, in part, on the uniqueness and persuasiveness of your presentation and point of view. You can bring in props and other creative elements. Be creative. Be personal. Be authentic.

 

In addition, we encourage you to choose a topic that will lend itself well to visual slide design, as the slide design is an element of elevating one’s presentations, and using slides is required.

 

Preparation and Practice
Please rehearse with a timer. These presentations have a specific time limit – four to five minutes. Time is important for many reasons. Not only do time limits force us to limit the focus of our speeches, they are also real world. Often in business life you will have a very short time to make an important pitch. Time is also vital to the logistics of this class. If your presentation goes long, your breakout group may not have enough time to properly critique each speaker, and your grade will suffer. In addition, we will expect you to give your presentation without notes, so please practice speaking through your slides extemporaneously until you feel very comfortable with the speech content.
