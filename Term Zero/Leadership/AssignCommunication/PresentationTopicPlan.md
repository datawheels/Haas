# Persuasive Presentation — Topic Plan: Calendar Planning (Generic)

6 candidate approaches, no product/app and no named public figures. Evaluated against the course framework: **vertical takeoff → appeal to minds and hearts → concrete, memorable call to action.**

Each approach is drafted as a **Key Message / Emotional Intention / Image** table (per `content/Screenshot 2026-07-21 at 7.42.53 PM.png` and Vid 3's delivery guidance). Vid 3 notes a flat run of "happy, excited, thrilled, pleased, good" is less interesting/memorable than genuine variety — each table below moves through a mixed emotional arc rather than staying positive throughout.

---

## Approach 1 — The Calendar Audit (interactive/participatory)

**Topic name:** "The 90% Calendar"

Make the audience the subject instead of watching a demo.

| Key Message | Emotional Intention | Image |
|---|---|---|
| [Vertical Takeoff] "Pull up your calendar right now. I'll wait." Genuine pause. | Put on the spot / self-conscious | None — direct address, no slide or a blank/dark slide |
| [Heart & Mind] "Now count — how many blocks are things *you* chose, vs. things that just showed up?" Reactive vs. proactive time framework — most calendars are 90%+ reactive. | Curious | Simple two-column diagram: Reactive vs. Proactive |
| [Heart & Mind] Tie to the shared Hass 20hrs/22-months commitment — everyone chose to be here. What happens to that intention if it never gets its own slot? | Uneasy | Blank/empty calendar grid graphic |
| [Heart & Mind] Quick reveal — most rooms, when asked to actually count, find only 1-2 proactive blocks in a typical week. | Surprised / slightly alarmed | A real (anonymized) calendar screenshot with reactive blocks greyed and proactive blocks highlighted |
| [Call to Action] "Turn one reactive block this week into a proactive one — replace it with time toward your Hass hours or one personal goal." | Resolved | Bold text slide, no imagery needed |

**Notes:** Highly interactive, low risk of feeling like a pitch, memorable because the audience *does* something. Needs confident pacing to hold the room during the opening pause.

## Approach 2 — The Time-Debt Framing

**Topic name:** "Time Debt: The Bill Nobody Sends You"

Reframe unscheduled goals as debt that compounds.

| Key Message | Emotional Intention | Image |
|---|---|---|
| [Vertical Takeoff] A goal you'd claimed for months but never scheduled — and the exact day it became undeniable you hadn't moved on it. | Embarrassed | Personal photo tied to the goal (e.g. unused gym bag, unread book) |
| [Heart & Mind] Analogy to financial debt — small unscheduled gaps compound weekly. | Intrigued | Simple "debt curve" chart: hours owed vs. hours allocated |
| [Heart & Mind] By year-end, a goal with zero recurring calendar presence has near-zero chance of completion. | Concerned | Same chart, curve widening over 52 weeks |
| [Heart & Mind] The relief of finally putting a number and a slot on it, instead of avoiding the goal. | Relieved | A single recurring block appearing on a calendar graphic |
| [Call to Action] "Pick your one goal with the most 'time debt.' Give it a recurring weekly slot before Sunday." | Motivated | Bold text slide with the deadline (Sunday) visible |

**Notes:** Strong, sticky metaphor; persuasive with just one simple chart. Needs the visual to be tight so the debt analogy lands inside 4-5 minutes.

## Approach 3 — Borrow Someone Else's Week

**Topic name:** "Copy the Week, Not the Trophy"

Reframe: people copy outcomes but rarely copy the actual weekly structure that produced them.

| Key Message | Emotional Intention | Image |
|---|---|---|
| [Vertical Takeoff] "Think of someone whose results you genuinely want. Do you actually know what their week looks like — or just the outcome?" | Challenged | Silhouette or blank outline (no named figure) |
| [Heart & Mind] Most people chase the result without ever studying the recurring weekly structure behind it. | Reflective | Simple "outcome vs. structure" two-box diagram |
| [Heart & Mind] A goal only becomes real once broken into scheduled, recurring actions — not because the goal changed, but because the calendar did. | Convinced | Goal → recurring action → calendar slot flow diagram |
| [Heart & Mind] A personal moment of admiring someone's outcome for months without ever asking what their actual week looked like. | Regretful | Personal photo or plain text slide |
| [Call to Action] "Find one person whose results you want. Ask them one question: what does your actual week look like? Block your own calendar to match it, starting with your Hass 20 hours." | Determined | Bold text slide |

**Notes:** Distinct emotional hook (aspiration/modeling). Asks the audience to act outside the room, which is less immediately verifiable than blocking a calendar live.

## Approach 4 — The Compounding Week

**Topic name:** "30 Minutes, 52 Weeks"

Small recurring choices, multiplied out, reveal outsized stakes.

| Key Message | Emotional Intention | Image |
|---|---|---|
| [Vertical Takeoff] Pick one small, dismissible recurring block — e.g. 30 minutes every morning on autopilot scrolling. | Amused / dismissive (about to be undercut) | Simple icon of a phone/clock |
| [Heart & Mind] Multiply it live: 30 min × 7 days × 52 weeks. Reveal the total in days, not hours. | Shocked | Large number reveal slide (the total, in days) |
| [Heart & Mind] A calendar isn't a record of one day — it's a template that repeats 52 times a year. | Uneasy | Calendar page repeating/tiling visual |
| [Heart & Mind] The moment you first did this math on your own week and didn't like the answer. | Sobered | Personal photo or plain text slide |
| [Call to Action] "Pick one recurring block you're on autopilot for. Do the math tonight. Replace it with something you actually chose, starting next week." | Resolved | Bold text slide |

**Notes:** Concrete, visual, easy to build suspense around the reveal. Pick the example carefully so the multiplied number is genuinely surprising, not obvious.

## Approach 5 — The Default Calendar

**Topic name:** "The Calendar You Never Chose"

A behavioral-economics angle: most of what's on your calendar, you never actually decided on.

| Key Message | Emotional Intention | Image |
|---|---|---|
| [Vertical Takeoff] "How many recurring events on your calendar did you actually choose — vs. auto-added by someone else's invite, or just always been there?" | Caught off guard | None — direct question, no slide |
| [Heart & Mind] Default bias — people rarely question what's already been placed on their calendar; it becomes invisible. | Curious | Simple "default vs. chosen" icon pair |
| [Heart & Mind] The calendar quietly fills with other people's priorities by default, leaving no room for your own unless you deliberately claim it. | Frustrated | Calendar graphic, mostly filled with grey "default" blocks |
| [Heart & Mind] The moment you realized a recurring commitment you'd never questioned was crowding out something that actually mattered. | Regretful | Personal photo or plain text slide |
| [Call to Action] "Find one default recurring event you never chose. Decline, shorten, or move it — and put something you actually chose in its place." | Empowered | Bold text slide |

**Notes:** Sharp, slightly contrarian framing; works well for a room with packed calendars. Lands best with a near-universal example (recurring status meetings, standing calls).

---

## Approach 6 — The Calendar as Contract

**Topic name:** "A Meeting With Yourself"

Treat your own calendar entries with the same weight as a commitment to someone else.

| Key Message | Emotional Intention | Image |
|---|---|---|
| [Vertical Takeoff] A moment you cancelled a personal commitment on your calendar without a second thought — but would never no-show a meeting with your boss. | Guilty | Personal photo or plain text slide |
| [Heart & Mind] "Why the difference?" People treat entries involving others as binding, and entries with only themselves as optional. | Reflective | Two-column comparison: "meeting with boss" vs. "meeting with myself" |
| [Heart & Mind] The fix isn't a new tool — it's deciding your own blocks carry the same weight as anyone else's meeting. | Convinced | Simple equals-sign diagram between the two calendar entry types |
| [Heart & Mind] The relief of the first time you didn't break a promise to yourself. | Relieved | Personal photo or plain text slide |
| [Call to Action] "Put your next personal goal block on the calendar right now, and treat it as unmissable as a meeting with your boss. Do not move it this week." | Determined | Bold text slide |

**Notes:** Simple, universal psychological hook, no visuals strictly required. Needs a sharp, specific personal story so the setup doesn't feel generic.

---

## Recommendation

**Approach 1 (Calendar Audit)** and **Approach 4 (Compounding Week)** are the strongest picks — both create a genuine in-room moment (the pause, the live multiplication) rather than just telling the audience something, and both end in a call to action they can execute immediately, not just agree with.

If you want the sharpest single emotional hook with minimal setup, **Approach 6 (Calendar as Contract)** is the simplest to deliver well in 4-5 minutes.
