# Overview — Persuasive Presentation: "The Calendar Doesn't Lie!"

## Assignment (from [Ask.md](Ask.md))
- Individual 4–5 minute persuasive presentation on a personal/timely topic, built around ~5 visual, low-text slides.
- Must end in a concrete **call to action** for the audience — not just awareness-raising.
- Slides support the speech; they are not the show (minimal text, minimal bullets, visual emphasis).
- Process: draft slides July 21–30, revise July 30–Aug 7; log tool/prompt usage in the bCourses-Rumi appendix throughout.
- Grading emphasizes authenticity, personal risk-taking, and persuasiveness of point of view.

## Topic
**Deliberate weekly planning vs. reactive drift**, using the EMBA workload as a live example: your calendar reveals your true priorities, and choosing to plan is a deliberate act.

## Draft Evolution
- [draft1.md](draft1.md) — raw first-pass narrative/notes (unedited voice, typos), containing the story, the 168-hour math, and the reactive-vs-deliberate framing.
- [draft2.md](draft2.md) — cleaned-up speaking script / rehearsal notes, organized into 5 slide-aligned sections:
  1. Title/Hook — do you know the weekly structure behind someone's results?
  2. Personal Story — *The Social Network* (2010), the failed startup attempt, missing framework of planning/execution/accountability.
  3. The Math Reveal — goal → why → calendar; 168 hours/week, 126 committed, 42 free, EMBA needs 20.
  4. Reactive vs. Deliberate — the strategic choice of what to cut, Sunday 30-min planning ritual.
  5. Call to Action — open your calendar now, block 30 minutes tomorrow evening.
