# Block 2 — Managerial Economics: Demand and Pricing

## Objectives
By the end of this block, you will be able to:
1. Understand the fundamental aspects of price optimization
2. Implement demand aggregation across markets
3. Identify the value of separating markets and price discrimination
4. Recognize frameworks for price discrimination, versioning, and quantity discounts

## Class 4 — Price Setting by a Monopolist [10/1]

We turn to how a monopolist sets prices: marginal revenue, marginal cost, and the markup rule, and how these translate into real-world pricing decisions.

- Read [Class 4/Why Empty Seats at Taylor Swift's Concerts Are Good for Business - WSJ.pdf](Class%204/Why%20Empty%20Seats%20at%20Taylor%20Swift%27s%20Concerts%20Are%20Good%20for%20Business%20-%20WSJ.pdf) and think about:
  1. Is it possible that profit-maximizing prices can result in empty seats?
  2. What is the effect of Ticketmaster's Verified Fan program on the possibility to scalp tickets?
- Read [Class 4/Seeking Perfect Prices, CEO Tears Up the Rules - WSJ.pdf](Class%204/Seeking%20Perfect%20Prices%2C%20CEO%20Tears%20Up%20the%20Rules%20-%20WSJ.pdf) and think about:
  1. The article is clear on the pricing scheme Parker Hannifin abandoned, but less clear on what replaced it. What factors now affect how Parker prices its parts? The article suggests the standard markup rule (35% in Parker's case) might make sense for a firm selling commodities, but not one selling a portfolio of niche products — if you were selling a commodity product, would a standard markup rule be a good strategy?
  2. Firms and managers are often concerned about market share. How is the new pricing scheme likely to change the market shares of Parker's products?

## Class 5 — Advanced Pricing [10/2]

We continue exploring optimal pricing by moving into more sophisticated strategies: group price discrimination, two-part tariffs, and market segmentation.

**Before Class**
- Read [Class 5/Bowling for $418 Surge Pricing Creeps Into Restaurants, Movies, Gym Class - WSJ.pdf](Class%205/Bowling%20for%20%24418%20Surge%20Pricing%20Creeps%20Into%20Restaurants%2C%20Movies%2C%20Gym%20Class%20-%20WSJ.pdf) and think about:
  1. Why might charging different prices at different times be a successful pricing strategy?
  2. The Bowlero CFO states that charging different prices based on day and time helps "simultaneously maximiz[e] guest happiness through reasonable wait times and maximiz[e] profitability by scaling price when the willingness to pay is there." Do some consumers "maximize their happiness" when they pay higher prices?
- Read [Class 5/On Orbitz, Mac Users Steered to Pricier Hotels - WSJ.pdf](Class%205/On%20Orbitz%2C%20Mac%20Users%20Steered%20to%20Pricier%20Hotels%20-%20WSJ.pdf) and think about:
  1. Do you think Orbitz has information to estimate the price elasticity of demand for hotels by Mac vs. PC users? If so, can the company use this information to target price promotions to only PC users?
  2. Do you think Orbitz's price discrimination strategy may be helping or harming consumers?
- Read [Class 5/NYT Costco Is Cracking Down on Membership Sharing 062823.pdf](Class%205/NYT%20Costco%20Is%20Cracking%20Down%20on%20Membership%20Sharing%20062823.pdf) and prepare to discuss:
  1. What problem did self-checkout lanes introduce that interfered with Costco's pricing strategy?
  2. How would you evaluate the costs and benefits of checking IDs at self-checkout versus eliminating self-checkout altogether?
